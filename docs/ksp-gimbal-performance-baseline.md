# KSP and Gimbal performance baseline: different workloads

The first live comparison runs on one Apple M4 Max (16 logical CPUs). KSP 1.12.5 uses Unity 2019.4.18f1, OpenGL, a visible 1920×1080 window, installed mods, and the opt-in [Continuum PlayerLoop profiler](profiling.md). Gimbal's current native three-part launch runs headless with no KSP or Unity. These measurements establish scale and a future test ladder; they do **not** establish a KSP-to-Gimbal speedup.

| KSP capture, 300 visible frames | Loaded world | Mean / p95 frame | Complete FixedUpdate parent mean | Simulated / capture wall | Achieved rate |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Mun orbit, normal time | One vessel; 62 parts, 45 rigidbodies, 60 joints, 117 colliders, stable topology | 16.707 / 23.680 ms (59.86 mean FPS) | 2.607 ms per rendered frame | 5.020 / 5.006 s | 1.003× |
| Kerbin high-speed atmospheric flight, requested 4× physics warp | One vessel; 196→195 parts, 110→109 rigidbodies, 144→143 joints, 328→324 colliders | 84.223 / 93.582 ms (11.87 mean FPS) | 25.956 ms per rendered frame | 95.440 / 25.188 s | 3.789× |
| Mun three-part coast, normal time | One vessel; 3 parts, 3 rigidbodies, 2 joints, 8 colliders, stable topology | 16.688 / 25.407 ms (59.92 mean FPS) | 1.808 ms per rendered frame | 5.000 / 5.006 s | 0.999× |

All three profiler reports completed with verified PlayerLoop boundary integrity, hook cleanup, zero dropped samples, and an unchanged source checkpoint. The atmospheric vessel lost one part during capture, so its topology was not fixed. Each fixed-parent mean uses 301 complete parent samples; its native-physics and managed-script children overlap that parent and must not be added to it. The frame interval includes rendering, scheduling and observer overhead; it is not a CPU-duration measurement.

`/usr/bin/time -l` also measured the *entire* KSP processes, including asset loading and shutdown. Mun orbit took 93.91 s wall with 96.36 s user and 20.64 s system CPU time (124.6% average process CPU), reaching 6.655 GB maximum RSS. Atmospheric flight took 115.92 s wall with 131.70 s user and 24.17 s system CPU time (134.5% average), reaching 6.837 GB maximum RSS. Those CPU and memory measurements are not isolated to the 5.006 s and 25.188 s profiler windows. They cannot explain the frame-time difference by themselves. Raw profiler receipts, process-time logs, and source hashes remain in the disposable local KSP lab instance; no saves, game files, local paths, or runtime state are committed here.

A warm release build of [Gimbal's three-part launch scenario](https://github.com/e0da/gimbal/pull/77) executed 1,200 fixed 0.1 s ticks (120 nominal simulated seconds), producing a byte-identical 719,648-byte replay artifact in repeated headless runs. `/usr/bin/time -l` reported 4.80–4.83 MB maximum RSS. Its first process measured 0.33 s wall; two warm processes displayed `0.00 s` wall, user and system at the tool's 0.01 s display resolution. Those warm timings are **below this timer's useful resolution**, so no precise throughput or CPU-utilization number is claimed. This scenario has capsule/tank/engine mass and propellant consumption, prograde thrust and central gravity, but no atmosphere, launch pad, guidance, joints, staging, secondary body, terrain, rendering or landing. It therefore does not reproduce a KSP row. The [many-craft J2 benchmark](https://github.com/e0da/gimbal/pull/81) is another isolated kernel workload, with independent point states rather than KSP loaded vessels.

The three-part KSP row uses a disposable copy of the stock Mun-orbit checkpoint trimmed to its lander cabin, small fuel tank, and LV-909 engine. Its source checksum remained unchanged. An opt-in profiler extension sampled the vessel's orbit position and velocity magnitudes, Mun radius and gravitational parameter alongside the normal frame context. At the first sample, KSP reported radius 232,624.674041954 m, orbital speed 530.574818248 m/s, radial speed 1.906125750 m/s, and Mun gravitational parameter 65,138,397,520.780693 m³/s². Five simulated seconds later, KSP reported radius 232,634.276023474 m, speed 530.553033924 m/s, and radial speed 1.938047210 m/s. [Gimbal's parameterized coast command](https://github.com/e0da/gimbal/pull/85), starting from those scalars and advancing 250 central-gravity steps of 0.02 s, ended at radius 232,634.284579774 m, speed 530.553015183 m/s, and radial speed 1.938047280 m/s: endpoint differences of 8.56 mm, 0.0187 mm/s, and 0.000070 mm/s respectively. The comparison rotates the initial state into a radial/tangential plane, which preserves these scalars for an airless central-gravity coast. This is a five-second trajectory agreement, not proof of long-term orbit, force, or joint equivalence.

The release Gimbal coast reported 0.20–0.50 ms of compute-only wall time across five warm 250-step runs. KSP's 5.006 s visible capture includes its renderer, active rigidbodies and joints, Unity, and frame scheduling. The runtimes therefore do not establish a comparable speedup ratio. The corresponding KSP process took 96.36 s wall including asset load and shutdown, 90.87 s user CPU plus 19.32 s system CPU, and 6.649 GB maximum RSS. Those are full-process metrics; capture-window CPU and RSS were not sampled. Gimbal's existing three-part vehicle still has different dry masses, propellants, and engine from the trimmed KSP craft. Neither side burned fuel in this coast rung.

Use an incremental mission ladder for a meaningful comparison: matched three-part launch and coast; staging and ascent to orbit; rendezvous and docking; transfer; descent, landing, ascent and return. At each rung, match initial state, body parameters, part mass/thrust/resource definitions, control inputs and duration. Check trajectory and event outcomes before comparing wall time, achieved simulated-time rate, frame-time distribution, process CPU/RSS and entity counts. Rendering must be matched or reported as a separate headless result. KSP's packed and unloaded vessels must not be counted as equivalent to fully integrated Gimbal craft.

Next, match dry mass, fuel, thrust, ISP and prograde control on the three-part craft, then compare a short burn and coast with trajectory and event error before timing. Sample CPU and RSS *during* each flight window. The 196-part atmospheric 4× case remains a later fidelity and performance target.
